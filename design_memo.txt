A binary tree works well for organizing doctors because it shows who reports to who in the hospital. Each doctor can have two doctors under them, one on the left and one on the right. This makes it easier to see how the hospital's management is set up. If the hospital needs to add a doctor, the tree can be updated to show where they belong.

Preorder, inorder, and postorder are three ways to go through a tree. Preorder looks at the parent first, then the children, which can be helpful when making a report that starts with the main doctor. Inorder looks at the left child, then the parent, and then the right child. Postorder looks at the children before the parent, which can be useful when finishing work on smaller departments before moving to the higher levels. Each method has a different purpose depending on how the information needs to be used.

A min-heap is useful in the emergency room because patients need to be seen based on how serious their condition is, not just when they arrived. In this project, a patient with an urgency of 1 gets priority over a patient with an urgency of 5. When a new patient is added or someone is removed from the queue, the heap rearranges the patients to keep the highest-priority patient at the front. This helps the system stay organized and makes it faster to find who needs attention next. Both data structures help the hospital manage information in a more organized way.
